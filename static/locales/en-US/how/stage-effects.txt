Make the whole stage look like old film, television, or water

Movies change how a whole scene looks: a flashback goes soft and bright, an old newsreel is scratched and gray, a television crackles with static, and anything underwater ripples and turns blue.
We can do the same by giving a @Stage a list of @Effect.

Here is a scene seen through water.

\
Stage(
	[Phrase('🐠') Phrase('🌿' place: Place(1m -1m))]
	effects: [Wave(0.15m) Tint(Color(55% 45 220°) 70%) Haze(40% Color(70% 30 200°))]
)
\

Each effect does one thing. @Wave ripples, @Tint washes everything in one color, and @Haze lays drifting fog over it.
Together they look like water. Effects apply in the order they are listed, so a later one changes the result of the ones before it.

There are ten to combine: @Blur, @Grade, @Tint, @Haze, @Grain, @Scanlines, @Wave, @Damage, @Flicker, and @Vignette.
Here is an old film: @Grade takes away the color, then @Damage, @Grain, and @Flicker make it worn and unsteady.

\
Stage(
	[Phrase('THE END')]
	effects: [Grade(contrast: 130% saturation: 0%) Damage(70% 50%) Grain(25%) Flicker(15% 2) Vignette(60%)]
)
\

And here is a television losing its signal.

\
Stage(
	[Phrase('channel 3')]
	effects: [Scanlines(40% speed: 1) Grain(50% size: 2) Flicker(20%)]
)
\

To use a look again and again, make a function that gives back its list of effects.
Its inputs become the look's own settings. This one gets murkier the deeper you go.

\
ƒ underwater(depth•%) [
	Wave(0.2m · depth)
	Tint(Color(55% 45 220°) depth)
	Blur(0.03m · depth)
]

Stage([Phrase('🐠')] effects: underwater(80%))
\

A look can also change with the state of a game.
Here the stage blurs and darkens at the edges when the player has only one life left, and clears again when they have more.

\
lives: 3 … ∆ Key() … lives - 1

Stage(
	[Phrase(lives → '')]
	effects: lives < 2 ? [Blur(0.05m) Vignette(70% Color(30% 80 30°))] []
)
\

Effects cover what is on the stage, but never its @Stage.overlay, so a score or a button stays clear to read.

Shaking the whole stage, like an earthquake, isn't an effect at all: a @Stage can move like any other output, so give it a @Sequence.

\
Stage([Phrase('🏠')] resting: Sequence.shake(0.5s count: 4x))
\

Some effects move quickly. When someone has asked their device to reduce motion, every effect holds still.
@Flicker never flashes faster than a few times a second, however fast you ask, because fast flashing can hurt people.
