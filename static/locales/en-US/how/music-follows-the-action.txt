Make the music follow the action

A game that plays one tune from start to finish feels flat. What if the music knew whether you were winning?

There's no way to *stop* a @Music — what stops it is not being on the @Stage. So keep a piece of state, and put the song that matches it on stage:

\
phase: 'menu' … ∆ Button() … (phase = 'menu') ? 'chase' 'menu'

theme: [1 ø 3 ø 5 ø 6 ø]

Stage([
	Phrase((phase = 'menu') ? '▶ press to begin' '🏃 run!' 1m)
	(phase = 'menu')
		? Music(Track(theme instrument: Instrument.bell) tempo: 72beats/min)
		Music(Track(theme instrument: Instrument.synth) tempo: 132beats/min)
])
\

Both songs are the same eight numbers. Only the instrument and the tempo change, and it already sounds like a different piece — because degrees say *which step of the scale*, not which pitch, so a @Music can reinterpret them. Hand it \scale: Music.harmonicMinor\ and the same tune turns sinister without a note being rewritten.

The other half is music that bends continuously with how things are going. Changing \tempo\ doesn't restart anything — it splices in at the next beat, so the piece speeds up without losing its place:

\
score: 0 … ∆ Button() … score + 1
urgency: (score ÷ 10).min(1)

Stage([
	Phrase('score \score\' 1m)
	Music(
		Track([1 5 8 5] instrument: Instrument.drums)
		tempo: (90 + (urgency · 90)) · 1beats/min
	)
])
\

Press enough times and the drums drive twice as hard as they started. A short, percussive note is what makes that audible — under long held notes a tempo change is hard to feel.
