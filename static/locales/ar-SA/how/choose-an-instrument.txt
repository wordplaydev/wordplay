Let your audience choose the instrument

Everything about a @Music can be decided while it plays, including who plays it. Hand the @Track a different @Instrument and the tune keeps its place — only the voice changes.

@Choice gives back the \name\ of whichever selectable output the audience picked, so a conditional is enough to turn that into an instrument:

\
picked: Choice()

Stage([
	Group(Row() [
		Phrase('🎹' selectable: ⊤ name: 'piano')
		Phrase('🎻' selectable: ⊤ name: 'violin')
	])
	Music(
		Track(
			[1 3 5 6 5 3]
			instrument: (picked = 'violin') ? Instrument.violin Instrument.piano
		)
	)
])
\

The two selectable phrases are what make this work — @Choice has nothing to read unless something on stage is \selectable: ⊤\🪲.

Two instruments fit in one conditional. A whole palette doesn't, and you'd end up writing the list twice: once for the buttons and once for the sound. Keep them together in a structure of your own instead, and build both from it:

\
•Voice(icon•'' sound•🔈)

voices•[Voice]: [
	Voice('🎹' Instrument.piano)
	Voice('🎺' Instrument.trumpet)
	Voice('🔔' Instrument.bell)
]

picked: Choice()
found: voices.find(ƒ(voice•Voice) voice.icon = picked)
current•Voice: found•ø ? voices[1] found

Stage([
	Group(Row() [
		:voices.translate(
			ƒ(voice•Voice) Phrase(voice.icon selectable: ⊤ name: voice.icon)
		)
	])
	Music(Track([1 3 5 6 5 3] instrument: current.sound))
])
\

Adding a fourth voice is now one line, and the button appears by itself. Note the \found•ø ?\🪲 guard: @List/find gives back \ø\ when nothing matches, which is what happens before anyone has chosen — and the fallback has to be a whole \Voice\, since that's what \current\ promises to be.
