Play a sound when something happens

A ding when the answer is right, a thud when something lands — a sound effect is a tiny @Music that plays once and gets out of the way.

Two things make it a sound effect rather than a song. \loop: ⊥\ tells the @Track to play its notes once instead of forever. And the note list itself comes from the moment: it holds notes when something just happened, and is empty the rest of the time.

\
tapped: ∆ Button()

Stage([
	Phrase('👆 tap me' selectable: ⊤ name: 'tap')
	Music(
		Track(tapped ? [1 5 8] [] instrument: Instrument.bell loop: ⊥)
		replay: tapped
	)
])
\

Don't worry that the list empties again on the very next evaluation — a note that has started always plays to its end. What you can't leave out is \replay\. Tap twice and the second tap produces exactly the same music as the first, so nothing changed and nothing would sound again; \replay\ is how you say /again, now/.

@Music wants a *momentary* condition here — one that's true only on the evaluation the event caused, like the \∆ Button()\ above. Something lasting, like \score > 10\, stays true forever after and would restart the sound on every evaluation.

Since the notes are just a list, you can decide them from *what* happened rather than always playing the same thing:

\
key: Key()
struck: ∆ key

Stage([
	Phrase(struck ? key '⌨️ press a key' 1m)
	Music(
		Track(
			struck ? [((key = ' ') ? 1 8)] []
			instrument: Instrument.piano
			loop: ⊥
		)
		replay: struck
	)
])
\

Notice that \∆ key\ is worked out on its own line and the value test \key = ' '\ happens separately. Written together as one condition, the sound would fire again on the next unrelated key while \key\ still read as a space.
